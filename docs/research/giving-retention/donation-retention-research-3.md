# Donation and accounting record retention: rest of the world (report 3)

started: 2026-10-11T19:28:37Z donation retention research 3 (read date 2026-10-11)
Plan: Europe and parts of Asia by me; Africa+Pacific by helper 1; Americas+territories+Middle East by helper 2 (two helpers, as instructed).

## Summary (read date 2026-10-11; facts, not legal advice)

- **Coverage:** 147 rows: all 129 UN member states not in reports 1 and 2, plus Puerto Rico, Bermuda, Cayman Islands, British Virgin Islands, Turks and Caicos, Aruba, Curacao, Sint Maarten, Jersey, Guernsey, Isle of Man, Gibraltar, Macau, Taiwan, Palestine, Kosovo, Cook Islands and Vatican City. Tables are grouped by who researched them, so a region may appear in more than one place.
- **Source quality is weaker than in reports 1 and 2.** Official sites were mostly unreachable through the search tool, and the tool returns summaries. **Almost every retention period comes from one 2019 academic survey (ARMA, "Retention of Accounting Records: A Global Survey", SECONDARY, a 2019 snapshot).** Where I could check an official page, the row says so. Only a handful of rows reach READ: Bulgaria (ministry page), Latvia (old translation of the law, marked outdated by its host), Albania and Turkey (data-protection laws), the OHADA 10-year rule (2000 text, read by the Africa helper; the 2017 text was seen only in a summary).
- **Periods found: 3 to 30 years for ordinary records.** Most countries sit at 5, 6, 7 or 10. Ten years is the norm across the 17 OHADA states in Africa (French-speaking Africa), most of the Middle East, and much of Europe east of Germany. Longer outliers: El Salvador 30 years (company books), Uruguay 20, Serbia 20 (financial statements), Iceland 25 (annual accounts), Afghanistan 15 (ledgers), China 15 (foreign-invested companies). Annual financial statements are kept permanently or until closure in Bosnia, Slovenia and Latvia; payroll 50 years in Bulgaria and Lithuania.
- **Donor-identifying records: NOT FOUND for most of these countries.** Where a rule was found: Estonia (charity files a yearly return listing each donor's name, ID code and amount), Dominican Republic (donor list and tax invoice), Panama (charity reports donor name, tax ID, date, amount), Gibraltar (charity keeps Gift Aid donor declarations), Jersey (Jersey Gift Support claims), China (receipt for every gift, even anonymous, kept on file). Most others have donor relief but I found no statement of what the charity must hold.
- **Data protection:** every EU and EEA state: GDPR Art 17(3)(b) (READ) lets an organisation keep data it must keep by law. Outside Europe the picture is patchy: laws with a clear "legal duty" exception that I could confirm in the text: Albania (Art 15(3)(b), READ), Turkey (Art 4(2)(d), 7(2), READ). Partly confirmed in secondary sources: China (data kept but frozen), Taiwan, Vietnam, Jersey, Russia. Many countries have no general law in force (e.g. Cambodia, Timor-Leste, Maldives, Turkmenistan, several Caribbean and Pacific states). Several new laws are not yet in force or their dates conflict (Belize, Guyana, Paraguay, Montenegro, Bosnia, Cambodia, Kyrgyzstan).
- **Nothing reliable on record retention:** Puerto Rico, Turks and Caicos, Aruba, Curacao, Sint Maarten, Palestine, Somalia, Cook Islands, Vatican City. Central African Republic: tax period not found (OHADA 10 years covers accounting).
- **Not checked at all:** state or provincial laws, religious-body exemptions, school or government-funded body rules, and whether any country in these tables has changed its law since 2019. Facts from memory are marked "from memory, unverified" in the rows.


## Helper 1 (Africa and Pacific): method notes
- Retention baseline for most rows is the ARMA Foundation 2019 global survey ("Retention of Accounting Records: A Global Survey", SECONDARY, 2019 snapshot, law may have changed since). Official pages were rarely reachable; where I confirmed something on an official or near-official page I say so.
- OHADA (Uniform Act on Accounting Law and Financial Information, 26 Jan 2017, replacing the 2000 Act): accounting books and supporting vouchers kept 10 years. Seen only as a summary on a Senegalese government document site (SUMMARY, https://www.vie-publique.sn/documents/11026/acte-uniforme-droit-comptable-information-financiere-ohada-2017); the article text (reported as Art. 24) and the start point were NOT read. ARMA 2019 cites OHADA Arts 19 and 24. OHADA members (17): Benin, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Chad, Comoros, Congo, Cote d'Ivoire, DR Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Mali, Niger, Senegal, Togo (https://www.ohada.org/en/history-of-ohada/ via search summary). Not members: Mauritania, Madagascar, Djibouti, Burundi, and the other states in my list.
- Data-protection column: "DPA fact sheet" = the country page at dataprotection.africa (a civil-society/vendor tracker, SECONDARY, pages mostly last updated 2022; it states what the law says in summary only). Corporate tax-relief facts from PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries (SECONDARY, vendor).

## Legend and shared sources (used in every table)

- **Strength:** READ = I read the law or official page. SUMMARY = an official or authoritative page seen only through a search summary. SECONDARY = law firm, accounting firm, NGO, news or academic survey.
- **ARMA 2019** = "Retention of Accounting Records: A Global Survey of Laws and Regulations" (ARMA International Educational Foundation, 2019, 200 countries). I read the entry for each country locally. It cites the laws but is **SECONDARY and a 2019 snapshot**; later law changes are not covered. URL: https://armaedfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/AIEF-Research-Paper-Retention-Global-Accounting.pdf
- **GDPR** = Regulation (EU) 2016/679. Art 17(3)(b) lets an organisation refuse erasure where processing "is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation which requires processing by Union or Member State law" (READ via https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/, a copy of the Regulation; official text https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj). Applies directly in every EU state, and in Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway through the EEA Agreement. National implementing acts were NOT checked unless named.
- "Donor-ID" column: yes = the charity or the tax rules need donor identity for relief; receipted = only for gifts that get a receipt or relief; no tax relief = none found for the donor kind named; not found = I found no reliable source.
