# Donation record retention - 40 more countries (read 11 October 2026)

Facts, not legal advice. Every "safe default" needs confirming by a local adviser.

## Summary

- **Periods found for tax or accounting records: 5 to 10 years.** 5 years: Colombia (minimum), Costa Rica, Mexico, Uganda, Tanzania, Egypt, Korea (donor-receipt log), Thailand (up to 7 on extension), Romania (current rule). 6: Chile, Trinidad and Tobago, Zimbabwe, Pakistan. 7: Barbados, Malawi, UAE, Israel, Malaysia, Hong Kong, Austria, Ecuador (sales vouchers). 8: Botswana. 10: Argentina, Peru (up to), Rwanda, Ethiopia, Morocco, Cameroon, Indonesia, Switzerland, Portugal, Belgium (tax), Japan (company law), Finland (financial statements, from memory). The count usually starts at the end of the tax or financial year, but a few start later (Romania: 1 July of the next year; Colombia: 1 January of the next year).
- **Rules that name the donor in the charity's own records (found):** Chile (numbered certificate plus donor book), Mexico (digital receipt; donor-identifying control for gifts in kind), Israel (receipt carries the donor ID number, reported to the tax authority from 1 January 2026), Austria (name and date of birth, reported yearly), Korea (per-donor issue log with name, ID number and address, 5 years), Japan (certified non-profits keep a donor list for 5 years), Malaysia (receipt shows donor name and ID or passport number), Portugal (receipt shows donor name and tax number), Finland (recipient reports donors to the tax office), Belgium (certificate sent through the tax office's system).
- **No tax relief for donors (or almost none):** UAE (individuals), Ecuador (general rule), Bahamas (no income tax), Cameroon (gifts not deductible except narrow cases).
- **NOT FOUND, whole country or main item:** Papua New Guinea (tax retention and receipts), Sri Lanka (tax retention and receipt rule), Bangladesh (official retention period; only an unofficial 3-year figure), Zambia (income-tax retention), Barbados (tax retention), Trinidad and Tobago (receipt rules), Ethiopia/Uganda/Tanzania/Zimbabwe/Malawi/Botswana/Cameroon (any rule that the charity must keep donor identity), Thailand (donation receipt rules), Fiji (donation records), Switzerland (donor records).
- **Data-protection law missing or not in force:** Pakistan (bill only), Papua New Guinea (bill only), Fiji (bill only), Trinidad and Tobago (only part in force per a 2013 source; current position not found), Bahamas (2025 replacement law reported but not confirmed in force), Sri Lanka (Act exists but parts are not yet in force), Bangladesh (Ordinance of 2025; some duties start 18 months after 6 Nov 2025).
- **Erasure exception for a legal duty to keep data:** written in the law for Argentina, Chile (from 1 Dec 2026), Barbados (bill text), Botswana (bill text), Ethiopia, UAE, Austria/Belgium/Portugal/Finland/Romania (EU rules), Israel (no general erasure right at all), Hong Kong, Korea, Japan (indirectly). Weak or unclear: Egypt (retained data may have to stop identifying the person), Ecuador, Costa Rica (no exception written), Zambia (no explicit refusal wording), Trinidad and Tobago, Morocco.
- **Source quality:** many official sites were blocked or scanned. Where only a law firm, a professional body or a news report was read, the table says so. Items marked "memory" are from memory and unverified.

## Notes

1. **No source stated a retention period only for donor identity.** In practice donor records follow the general tax or accounting period. The exceptions are Korea (5 years from receipt issue) and Japan (5 years for the donor list).
2. **Egypt, Costa Rica, Ecuador:** the data-protection wording can cut against keeping identifying data. Egypt art. 4 says retained data must not stay identifying once the purpose ends. These three need a local lawyer before the portal refuses an erasure request.
3. **Israel** has no general right to erase accurate data, so the question rarely arises there.
4. **Colombia:** two readings of the retention period (5 years from 1 January, or until the return can no longer be challenged). Use the longer.
5. **Switzerland and Austria** have tax relief but the charity-side duty to keep donor details was not found (Switzerland) or is tied to yearly reporting (Austria).
6. **Botswana, Barbados:** erasure sections were read from bills, not the enacted acts.
7. **Chile:** the new law comes into force 1 December 2026; until then the older law applies and was not read.
8. **Helpers:** three helper agents gathered the Americas, Africa and Middle East; I did Europe, Asia and Pacific. Search results were mostly summaries, so "read" means a summary or page was read, not that every statute was opened in full.
